# AIE Corporate Cartoon

Use this skill when creating or developing AIE corporate cartoons, workplace satire, or the Murphy’s Law: South Asian Office Edition series.

## Signature

- Use a warm contemporary South Asian workplace context: chai, tiffin boxes, indoor plants, glass meeting rooms, mixed office/home working, monsoon details, and culturally grounded everyday texture where relevant.
- Keep the humour intelligent, affectionate, and recognisable. Satirise corporate jargon, managerial ego, bureaucracy, technical failure, performative wellness, and workplace contradictions—not South Asian identity.
- Keep the recurring anchor character recognisable: a South Asian woman with shoulder-length dark hair, expressive eyes, and a green kurta/top. Her green clothing is the visual continuity cue.
- Let her evolve like a long-running newspaper-comic protagonist. She may be an employee, observer, mentor, rebel, reluctant leader, pragmatist, dreamer, caregiver, or workplace changemaker. Vary her ambitions, relationships, setbacks, choices, and victories; do not reduce her to a static mascot or a single joke.

## Panel choice

- Choose two panels for a compact setup-and-punchline or a clear Murphy’s Law reversal.
- Choose four panels when the premise needs escalation, a reversal, character development, or a warmer final beat.
- Keep the panel count purposeful. Do not stretch a one-joke premise into four panels.

## Writing and composition

- Build each cartoon around one concrete workplace contradiction and one memorable final line.
- Prefer visual escalation: show the absurdity in the room, calendar, screen, form, meeting, or office object before explaining it.
- Use short, legible speech bubbles and captions. Preserve exact supplied wording when the user provides copy; avoid adding unnecessary readable text.
- For generated raster art, use the available image-generation workflow. Ask for clear gutters, consistent characters, left-to-right readability, large legible text, and no watermark.
- Save project-bound final artwork in the workspace and report its path.

## Murphy’s Law series

For the Murphy’s Law: South Asian Office Edition series, use a consistent series caption or visual marker when appropriate. Typical structure:

1. A reasonable expectation or simple office task.
2. A sudden, universal workplace failure or reversal.
3. The green-shirt character’s calm, precise observation as the punchline.

Keep the ending funny and humane. The protagonist can outmanoeuvre bad systems or pompous behaviour, but avoid cruelty, humiliation, or harm.
